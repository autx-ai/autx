# Independent review, 30 October 2026

Before promoting the AUTX AI Visibility Check, AUTX had it reviewed by a reviewer outside the team that built it: the code, the live site, the sample report and the checkout page. The reviewer made no changes to the live service, bought nothing, called no AI model and contacted no customer. This page sums up what the review found and where each finding stands.

## What the review said to keep

The review said to keep the free check and the $49 one-time report. The site already had a clear offer, a real sample, a visible price, a free check that needs no email, and Stripe's hosted checkout. The review's advice: before more traffic, make the results more trustworthy and the report easier to act on, rather than add more models.

## Fixed on 30 October 2026

| Finding | What changed |
|---|---|
| A report could count as complete with most of its answers missing. In the review's test, 6 of 78 planned answers came back and the report still read complete, because the count dropped the calls skipped at the spending cap. | Every planned call now counts. A report needs at least 60% of all planned calls answered. Then, leaving out any model that got the customer questions but answered none of them, it needs at least 90% of the remaining calls answered, and at least 80% within each of those models and each kind of question. A report that falls short runs again, and AUTX refunds an order that is still incomplete after three attempts. The rule is on [How it works](https://autx.ai/how-it-works/). |
| A lost counter could hide a sale from AUTX's own revenue report. In a test, the reviewer stored a $49 order while the counter storage failed, and the report then showed $0. | The revenue report now takes its totals from the stored orders themselves and shows any gap against the counters. |
| The home page was wider than a phone screen: 469px wide on a 390px phone, because of a decorative glow behind the headline. | The page now fits at 360, 390, 768 and 1440px. |
| How it works stated an older completeness rule. | The page now states the current rule, with its numbers taken from the code. |

## Open, in order

1. **Fair comparisons.** Two of the six models come from OpenAI, and Gemini answers without web search, so pooling the answers from all six models in one headline gives OpenAI double weight and mixes web results with model memory. The report will group results by company, show Gemini (which answers without web search) apart, and hold competitors to the same name rules as the business.
2. **A report an owner can act on.** Today the report's first page leads with technical model labels, and the actions start on page three. The next version will open with a two-page owner summary: what happened, the three things to do first, and who can do each one. It will add a separate brief for the owner's web developer.
3. **Sound technical advice.** The fix kit gives AI training crawlers the same access as the crawlers that power AI search. A business does not need to allow training to be recommended, so the fix kit will offer the two as separate choices. Google [says its search does not use llms.txt](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), so the fix kit will make that file optional. Advice will say what was observed and what still needs checking.
4. **Site, checkout and measurement.** A shorter home page; a short details step on autx.ai before Stripe, so no one pays without saying which business to check; and measurement that follows a visit from the free check to a paid report without collecting personal details.
